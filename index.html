What Aoze does

There is a workspace for images, one for clips and one for conversions, and each shows only the options that fit your file.

Image & GIF

  • Upscale by 1.6×, 2×, 4× or 8×
  • Noise reduction and clean-up
  • Compression, with an exact size limit
  • GIFs keep their timing and transparency
  • Discord sizes: avatar and banners

Opens PNG, JPG, WebP, BMP, TIFF and GIF.

Clips

  • Enhancement: Sharpen or Clean-up
  • Upscale up to 4×
  • Smooth Motion to 60 or 120 FPS, fluid even on anime drawn on twos
  • Motion blur and Speed: slow, fast, ramps
  • Smart Reframe to 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5
  • HDR, LUTs (.cube) and Beat Sync
  • Compression, with the audio kept

Opens MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM and AVI.

Convert

  • Clip to GIF
  • GIF to MP4
  • GIF to PNG frames
  • Images to GIF

You choose the FPS, width, quality and size limit.

Every option is explained in the README, and the video engine in its own notes.

Made with editors in mind

The settings that matter for a clip you're about to post, and nothing you have to fight with.

2460FPS

Smooth Motion

Real in-between frames, up to 120 FPS. It never blends two shots across a cut.

Motion blur and Speed

Blur made from the motion itself, like a camera shutter. Slow, fast or ramped speed, eased, with the sound following.

Smart Reframe

16:9 to 9:16 (or 1:1, 4:5) without losing the subject: the frame follows it smoothly, shot by shot.

Color

HDR kept or rebuilt as HDR10, LUTs (built-in looks or your .cube files), and simple Look sliders.

One click

The TikTok Smooth preset

Sharper, 60 FPS, a motion blur that suits speed ramps and a touch of colour, all at once. Hover a preset in Aoze to see what it turns on.

Fast, even on a modest PC

Aoze reads your hardware and adapts to it, from a small laptop to a gaming PC.

GPU acceleration

Works on any Vulkan GPU: NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, integrated. Without one, it falls back to the CPU. Slower, but it works.

Hardware encoding

At startup Aoze tests NVENC, AMF and Quick Sync, and uses the first one that works on your PC.

Tuned to your PC

Tile size, threads and memory use are chosen from your graphics card and free memory, before every run.

Leaves room for you

In Balanced mode the engines run at lower priority, so your PC stays usable while Aoze works.

Your files stay on your PC

Everything is processed on your computer. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and Aoze collects no usage data. The only connection the app makes on its own is the update check on GitHub, and you can turn it off.

If Windows doesn't have Microsoft's WebView2 runtime yet, the installer downloads it. Links such as GitHub open in your browser. Privacy policy

Before you install Aoze

Windows will most likely show a warning during installation. That's normal, and here is why.

Why the warning?

For Windows to recognise who made a program, the author has to buy a “code signing” certificate. Aoze is free and doesn't have one yet. So Windows doesn't know who published it, and warns you just in case. It doesn't mean the file is dangerous, only that Windows can't check its author yet.

What you may see

  1. In your browser: a message like “this file isn't commonly downloaded”. Choose “Keep”.
  2. When you open the file: a blue screen saying “Windows protected your PC”, with “Unknown publisher”. Click “More info”, then “Run anyway”.
  3. More rarely, your antivirus: may block the file. Don't turn your antivirus off: write to me and we'll look at it together.

What you can check

  • The file comes straight from Aoze's official GitHub page.
  • The source code is public (MIT License): anyone can read it.
  • Aoze collects no personal data and needs no account. Your rights (GDPR) are explained in the privacy policy.
  • Installing needs no administrator rights, and Aoze uninstalls from Settings → Apps.
